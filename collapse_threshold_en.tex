\begin{abstract}
We propose a modification of quantum mechanics through the introduction of the \CollapseRule{}: the Born weight of a branch is quantized in steps no smaller than $\varepsilon=1/\Ost$, so a branch with weight below~$\varepsilon$ does not exist, and outcome probabilities are counted among the existing branches. Objectivation occurs when the residual coherence falls below the same threshold~$\varepsilon$. The threshold is set by the finite number of binary distinctions within a causal patch: $\Ost=3.5\times 10^{120}$. The wall of equiprobable branching and the RCS limit lie no higher than about $400$ qubits; where exactly is for experiment to determine. Shor's algorithm stops working once the counting register is wider than the wall: for known schemes this means RSA moduli longer than about $800$~bits. The non-unitarity of truncation and selection permits non-conservation of energy, superluminal information transfer, and selection of the correct candidate in a single run for a search up to $400$~bits wide. An experimental test on a single processor is proposed, for which about $410$ qubits are certainly sufficient.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} objective collapse; objectivation; operationalism; vanishing of branches; residual coherence; einselection; computational limit; Shor's algorithm; superluminal telephone; CMB frame

\section{Introduction}

Standard quantum mechanics permits unitary decoherence: the reduced entropy of a subsystem grows, the global entropy is zero, and the phase is recorded in correlations with the environment and is recoverable (Loschmidt echo~\cite{swingle2018}). A classical bit is thereby not born \emph{objectively}: the outcome is definite only as an interpretation, not as a fact.

The Copenhagen interpretation separates a microscopic system that is in a superposition from a classical apparatus, but does not say where the Heisenberg cut lies and does not fix a numerical boundary. Decoherence and einselection select a pointer basis and explain the stability of macroscopic pointers~\cite{zurek2003}, yet by themselves they do not specify at what branching width a superposition remains physically distinguishable. The aim of this work is to locate that cut quantitatively and to work out the consequences.

The standard theory does not set a minimum nonzero value for density-matrix elements and probability weights. Decoherence can reduce coherences without bound, and sequential branching and the spatial tails of the wave function create arbitrarily small nonzero weights. If such quantities are treated as independently distinguishable physical data and stored with a fixed absolute resolution, the required bit depth grows linearly with time for exponential decay and quadratically with distance for a Gaussian tail. Without a lower threshold it is unbounded and, given a sufficiently long evolution, exceeds any finite information limit of the region, in particular the Bekenstein holographic bound~\cite{bousso2002}. Mathematically, arbitrarily small coefficients are admissible, but storing and distinguishing them physically at finite information capacity is impossible. Hence the idea of an information threshold below which values are physically indistinguishable.

The information that can be obtained and verified experimentally is finite in two independent senses. The first limit is set by quantum uncertainty: incompatible physical quantities cannot be measured simultaneously with arbitrarily high precision. The second sets the maximum number of experiments---questions that nature answers. It is tied to the finiteness of the causal patch---the maximal causally closed region accessible to one observer. The cosmological budget~$\Ost$ of that region permits only a finite number of operations over its entire future~\cite{lloyd2000,lloyd2002,banks2001,krauss2004}. Infinite information is therefore accessible neither through measurement precision nor through the number of physical operations by which it can be obtained.

Ideas of finite precision and of a threshold have been discussed before. Buniy, Hsu, and Zee linked the finiteness of distinguishable states to the discreteness of Hilbert space and the quantization of its coefficients~\cite{buniy2005}, and then allowed wave-function components with norm below a discreteness threshold to be excluded in order to eliminate ``maverick branches'' and restore Born's rule~\cite{buniy2006}. This threshold was not fixed: it depended on the characteristic energy or the system size and the number of subsystems. In an algorithmic approach to quantum physics, Ozhigov cut off small amplitudes: given an available computational resource (algorithm steps or memory cells), amplitudes below the inverse square root of that resource, i.e.\ probabilities below its reciprocal, are set to zero; a fixed amplitude quantum leaves only finitely many nonzero components and is used to describe measurement and to recover Born's rule~\cite{ozhigov2004}. For a finite amplitude precision~$\mu$, Srikanth obtained a memory overflow ${\cal M}_{\max}=\mu\cdot 2^{\mu/2}$ bits as entanglement grows: unitarity becomes computationally unstable, and an information transition makes the system jump into a less complex factorized state according to the Born rule, with the basis taken from decoherence~\cite{srikanth2003,srikanth2006}. Davies compared the cosmological information bound $I_{\mathrm{universe}}\sim10^{122}$ bits with the number of complex coefficients of a generic $N$-qubit state and obtained the scale $N\approx\log_2 I_{\mathrm{universe}}\approx400$~\cite{davies2007}. Davies's number~$400$ follows from the hypothesis that the cost of a classical description of the state limits its physical existence. Del Santo and Gisin introduced a finite informational precision of physical quantities and discussed a collapse-like actualization of undefined digits, but in the context of classical indeterminism rather than quantum branch weights~\cite{delsanto2019}. In the Montevideo interpretation, Gambini, Garc\'ia-Pintos, and Pullin include the gravitational limit on the precision of clocks and rods: an event occurs when the residual interference can no longer be distinguished from a reduction (undecidability, not FAPP)~\cite{gambini2011}. Harlow and Hayden showed that quantum gedanken experiments with black holes may be computationally inaccessible within the available time~\cite{harlow2013}.

The new proposal of this work is the \CollapseRule{}. The work is written within an instrumentalist framework: a physical description contains only what can be distinguished by experiment. This does not say what the indistinguishable quantities are equal to, so we introduce the postulate of limited description: within a causal patch no more than $\Ost$ binary distinctions are executable, and the Born weights of branches are multiples of a quantum no smaller than $\varepsilon=1/\Ost$. The rule acts on the full density matrix of the entangled cluster in the causal patch, in the einselection basis, without a system/environment split. A branch with $p_i<\varepsilon$ cannot be represented in such a description and vanishes. A coherence with $C\le\varepsilon$ vanishes in the same way---this is objectivation: the full matrix becomes a proper mixture, and a single outcome exists but is unknown. The threshold $\varepsilon$ sets the resolution limit of the entire region and is not divided among individual carriers as a quota. First a mnemonic estimate of the threshold is given from the memory capacity of the causal patch; it is then rejected in favor of counting executable distinctions (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}).

\section{Holographic Estimate of the Threshold}
\label{sec:holographic}

Consider a thought experiment: at the center is an atom that emits a photon of wavelength~$\lambda$. The photon departs in all directions at once: a spherical superposition of directions, without slits. Around it is a spherical screen of radius~$R$ with detectors over its entire surface. The number of distinguishable detection outcomes is the area of the sphere divided by the cell area~$\lambda^2$ (as the number of microstates):
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{cell}}(R,\lambda) \;=\; \frac{4\pi R^2}{\lambda^2}.
\end{equation}
Each cell is one individual outcome, ``photon detected here.'' In the cell basis the diagonal of the reduced~$\rho$ gives the probabilities of these outcomes; the off-diagonal elements give the coherence between directions. We count only the outcomes: how many diagonal addresses the sphere has.

\textbf{Limiting sphere.} The maximal radius is the de~Sitter horizon~$R_{\mathrm{dS}}$, to which the cosmological event horizon asymptotically tends in the future; the minimal wavelength is the thermal length at the Planck temperature~$T_P$:
\begin{equation}
R_{\max} \;=\; R_{\mathrm{dS}}, \qquad
\lambda_{\min} \;=\; \frac{\hbar c}{k_B T_P} \;=\; \ell_P,
\end{equation}
where $\ell_P=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}$ is the Planck length. The area of the limiting sphere is $A_{\mathrm{dS}}=4\pi R_{\mathrm{dS}}^2$. Therefore, at a resolution area~$\ell_P^2$, the number of binary cells on it is
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}
\;=\; \frac{A_{\mathrm{dS}}}{\ell_P^2}
\;=\; \frac{4\pi R_{\mathrm{dS}}^2}{\ell_P^2}
\;\approx\; 1.3\times 10^{123}.
\end{equation}
Assigning one binary detection fact, ``yes'' or ``no,'' to each cell corresponds to $1.3\times 10^{123}$~bits. The resulting quantity is of the order of the holographic capacity~\cite{bousso2002}: the de~Sitter entropy~\cite{gibbonshawking1977}, expressed in bits, equals
\begin{equation}
\frac{S_{\mathrm{dS}}}{\ln 2}
\;=\; \frac{A_{\mathrm{dS}}}{4\ell_P^2\ln 2}
\;=\; \frac{N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}}{4\ln 2}
\;\approx\; 4.8\times 10^{122}\ \text{bits}.
\end{equation}
Here $S_{\mathrm{dS}}$ denotes the dimensionless entropy in nats. The two estimates differ only by the factor~$4\ln 2$. The cells form an einselection basis in which the entire reduced~$\rho$ is written. The quantity~$N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}$ estimates the \emph{memory capacity} of the causal patch: how many binary facts can be stored in it. This estimate will be rejected in \hyperref[sec:computational]{\S~\ref*{sec:computational}} in favor of a different count---the number of \emph{executable} binary distinctions~$\Ost$. Until then, it is useful to remember the order of magnitude $1/N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}\sim 10^{-123}$ as a mnemonic scale, but not as the working threshold of the model.

\section{Cosmological Computational Limit}
\label{sec:cosmological-limit}

The calculation is carried out in the flat $\Lambda$CDM model: the FRW metric with $\Omega_k=0$, $\Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda+\Omega_r=1$; the radiation contribution in the present epoch is negligible ($\Omega_r<0.01\%$).

We take the Hubble horizon $R=c/H$, the radius of the apparent horizon in flat FRW, and the second Friedmann equation for the energy density~$\rho$ and pressure~$p$
\begin{equation}
\dot H = -\frac{4\pi G}{c^2}(\rho+p).
\end{equation}
Horizon growth rate:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dR}{dt}
= -c\,\frac{\dot H}{H^2}
= \frac{4\pi G}{c^3}(\rho+p)\,R^2.
\end{equation}
Then $\rho=\rho_m+\rho_\Lambda$, $p_m=0$, $p_\Lambda=-\rho_\Lambda$, whence $\rho+p=\rho_m$. Therefore, in the adopted approximation, the entropy change and the available operations are determined by the rest energy of non-relativistic matter; the dark-energy contribution vanishes because $\rho_\Lambda+p_\Lambda=0$. For a flat universe $\rho_m=\Omega_m\cdot 3c^4/(8\pi G R^2)$, and
\begin{equation}
\frac{dR}{dt} = \frac{3}{2}\,c\,\Omega_m.
\end{equation}

The entropy of the apparent horizon is $S=\pi k R^2/\ell_P^2$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dS}{dt}
= \frac{2\pi k R}{\ell_P^2}\frac{dR}{dt}
= \frac{3\pi k c^4}{\hbar G}\,\Omega_m R.
\end{equation}
The matter energy inside the horizon is $E_m=c^4\Omega_m R/(2G)$. Lloyd's limit~\cite{lloyd2000,lloyd2002}
\begin{equation}
\dot N_m = \frac{2E_m}{\pi\hbar} = \frac{c^4\Omega_m R}{\pi\hbar G}.
\end{equation}
Dark energy produces no entropy ($\rho_\Lambda+p_\Lambda=0$). The ratio
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dot S}{k\,\dot N_m} = 3\pi^2
\end{equation}
--- $3\pi^2$~nats (about $42.7$~bits) per orthogonal matter operation. $\Omega_m$ and~$R$ cancel: the relation is holographic and epoch-independent while matter and~$\Lambda$ dominate.

The de~Sitter entropy is finite; its value in nats, $S_{\mathrm{dS}}/k$, is given above (\hyperref[sec:holographic]{\S~\ref*{sec:holographic}}). The identity $S_{\mathrm{dS}}-S=S_{\mathrm{dS}}\Omega_m$ is exact: $R_H^2=R_{\mathrm{dS}}^2\Omega_\Lambda$ for a flat universe without radiation. Remaining orthogonal operations
\begin{equation}
\Ost(t)
= \int_t^\infty \dot N_m\,dt'
= \frac{S_{\mathrm{dS}}-S}{3\pi^2 k}
= \frac{S_{\mathrm{dS}}\Omega_m}{3\pi^2 k}
\approx 3.5\times 10^{120}
\end{equation}
The value given here is for the present epoch at $\Omega_m=0.315$ and $H_0=67.4$~km/s/Mpc~\cite{planck2020}. The integral is finite because the horizon asymptotically tends to~$R_{\mathrm{dS}}$. The calculation uses the Hubble horizon; with the cosmological event horizon, which is now larger by~$15\%$, the remainder is about three times smaller and the limit on the number of qubits (\hyperref[sec:computational]{\S~\ref*{sec:computational}}) is lower by~$1.7$. This does not affect the conclusions, since this limit is an upper bound. In a homogeneous Friedmann--Robertson--Walker (FRW) cosmology the quantity~$\Ost(t)$ is the same for comoving observers at the same cosmological time.

As~$\Omega_m$ decreases, $\Ost(t)$ decreases: at recombination $\Ost\approx 1.1\times 10^{121}$, now $\Ost\approx 3.5\times 10^{120}$, and in the asymptotic future $\Ost(t)\to 0$. On laboratory timescales the change in~$\Omega_m$ can be neglected.

This calculation refers to matter that disperses homogeneously. The wall then descends slowly: $N_w\approx\log_2\Ost$ loses one qubit in about~$4$~billion years, and in the matter era it was no higher than~$402$ qubits. The remainder tends to zero only in an empty region; there, not a single particle is left after about $1.2\times10^{12}$~years, and the question of superpositions no longer arises. Real matter clumps. A gravitationally bound mass~$M$ stays inside the horizon and keeps it from growing to the de~Sitter one: the entropy of the horizon stays below~$S_{\mathrm{dS}}$ by $2\pi k\,McR_{\mathrm{dS}}/\hbar$~\cite{bousso2002}. In this case it is natural to count the remainder from this entropy difference; it then tends not to zero but to $2McR_{\mathrm{dS}}/(3\pi\hbar)$. For the Local Group ($M\sim3\times10^{12}M_\odot$) this is about~$10^{111}$, so the wall does not descend below about~$370$ qubits. The threshold is thus stable: over the next billions of years it changes by fractions of a qubit.

\section{Computational Estimate of the Threshold}
\label{sec:computational}

In quantum Darwinism, objectivity arises when information about the state of the system is reproduced many times in independent fragments of the environment~\cite{zurek2009}; this picture develops Wheeler's ``It from Bit''~\cite{wheeler1989}. A binary distinction is understood here in Wheeler's sense---a yes/no answer to a posed question, i.e.\ an act rather than a stored record.

The quantities in \hyperref[sec:holographic]{\S~\ref*{sec:holographic}} and \hyperref[sec:cosmological-limit]{\S~\ref*{sec:cosmological-limit}} measure different things. $N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}$ is the memory capacity of the region: how many binary facts fit in it. $\Ost$ is the number of orthogonal operations: how many binary distinctions are executable in it. The ratio $12\pi^2/\Omega_m\approx376$ states how many times one count exceeds the other; it is not a correction to one of them. The same gap between the horizon entropy and the processable budget was noted by Krauss and Starkman~\cite{krauss2004}: their limit on the energy that can be harvested in de~Sitter space gives the order~$10^{120}$, but not an identity for~$\Ost$. Hereafter the count~$\Ost$ is adopted: physically, a branch exists insofar as it can be distinguished from absence, not insofar as it can be stored.

Comparing the remainder of operations with the number of binary cells on the limiting sphere, we obtain
\[
\frac{N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}}{\Ost}
=\frac{12\pi^2}{\Omega_m}
\sim 4\times 10^2.
\]
Therefore the mnemonic scale $1/N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}\sim 10^{-123}$ overestimates the resolvability: there is still enough memory, but not enough operations to distinguish all the addresses. On a logarithmic scale the same factor lowers the recorded limit from $\log_2 N_{\mathrm{cell,max}}\approx 409$ to $N_*=\log_2\Ost\approx 400.44$ (written as~$400.4$ below; the calculations use the exact value), i.e.\ by about $8.6$ qubits. Davies had already noted the same order~$400$~\cite{davies2007} by comparing the $2^N$ coefficients of an entangled state with $I_{\mathrm{universe}}\sim10^{122}$; that is a count of Hilbert-space dimension and memory capacity, not of executable distinctions, and it supplies no truncation rule. The working resolution is limited by the number of executable distinctions, not by memory capacity.

The quantity~$\Ost$ is not a consumable quota distributed among devices, but the total number of binary answers the causal patch can give over its entire future. The quantum of weight is the share of one such answer:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \frac{1}{\Ost}\approx 3\times 10^{-121}.
\end{equation}
An outcome with weight below~$\varepsilon$ gets less than one answer, that is, it never occurs. This is an estimate of scale, not a derivation of the exact value. $\Ost$ is computed from the Lloyd limit, that is, at the maximal rate of computation; real matter works more slowly, and one repetition of an experiment costs many operations. Therefore $1/\Ost$ is a lower bound on the quantum, and $N_*=\log_2\Ost\approx400$ is an upper bound on the position of the wall, which we denote~$N_w$. The dependence is logarithmic: each factor of~$1000$ in the number of available answers shifts the wall by~$10$ qubits. From below, the window is bounded by experiment: RCS on~$67$ and~$83$ qubits shows no wall~\cite{morvan2024,gao2025}. We expect the wall to lie in the upper part of the window, closer to~$400$ qubits. All estimates below are tied to the upper bound $N_w=N_*$; if the wall lies lower, the estimates for circuits shift with it, since they depend only on the difference $N-N_w$. The quantum~$\varepsilon$ characterizes the resolving power of the entire region, has the same value for every entangled system, and does not grow with the number of devices. It is a property of the causal patch, not of the laboratory apparatus: the local environment determines which branches and coherences are tested, but not the value of~$\varepsilon$.

The threshold does not forbid improbable series. A particular series of~$500$ coin tosses has probability $2^{-500}<\varepsilon$, and such series occur in every experiment. This is a combinatorial probability: the product of the weights of five hundred separate events, each of weight~$1/2$ (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}). The same holds for a $500$-bit file written bit by bit. The threshold applies to a coherent probability: the weight of one term of a superposition, which exists as a whole.

\section{\CollapseRule}
\label{sec:rules}

Postulate: a description of a physical state contains no more than~$\Ost$ independently distinguishable elements (\hyperref[sec:computational]{\S~\ref*{sec:computational}}), and the weight of each branch is an integer number of quanta $\varepsilon=1/\Ost$. A weight smaller than one quantum cannot be represented: there is no such branch in the description. This is not an action on the state but an absence: the branch is not removed, it vanishes. Only an existing branch can occur in a measurement, so outcome probabilities are counted among the existing branches. For coherence the same postulate yields objectivation: indistinguishable interference is not retained in the description, the off-diagonal elements of the full~$\rho$ are set to zero, and the description is a proper mixture of existing branches. The rule takes the full density matrix of all entangled particles and acts on it as a whole, without a system/environment split.

We introduce an integer micromodel that specifies nonnegative integers $n_i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, $\sum_i n_i=\Ost$; the weights lie on the grid $n_i/\Ost$ (for the upper bound; at $N_w<N_*$ the grid step is $2^{-N_w}$). Between zero and $\varepsilon=1/\Ost$ there is no admissible value, so the excess is shed by the vanishing of branches rather than by coarse-graining all the weights. The closest computational analogue is fixed-point arithmetic: a number smaller than the grid step gives machine zero (underflow). The analogy is incomplete: in a computer the loss of a small term does not affect the other numbers, whereas here probability is counted only among the existing branches, so the share of each of them grows. The full dynamical model is discussed in \hyperref[sec:discussion]{\S~\ref*{sec:discussion}}.

The \CollapseRule{} implements this postulate for einselection objects within a causal patch with resolving power~$\varepsilon$. By ``description'' we mean not the unitary circuit that prepared the state, but what experiment can distinguish afterwards: the Born weights of branches and the subsystem correlations that can be read from measurement statistics (\hyperref[sec:computational]{\S~\ref*{sec:computational}}).

\paragraph{Branches and two kinds of probability.}
\label{sec:systems}
A branch is one of the terms that make up the state and exist simultaneously. In Feynman's terms these are interfering alternatives~\cite{feynmanhibbs1965}. After objectivation there are no branches: there is an outcome.

For a single particle the branches are its alternatives in whatever the interaction distinguishes: energy level, spin projection, position. The branches in position are cells the size of the de~Broglie wavelength that corresponds to the characteristic momentum of the state. The state contains nothing finer: a measurement that localizes the particle more precisely imparts momentum to it and is a different operation with its own branches (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}).

In a composite state a small probability arises in two ways. In the first, the state is a product, $|\Psi\rangle=\bigotimes_a|\psi_a\rangle$. Each factor has its own state and its own branches, and the probability of a joint string is the product of their weights. In the second, the state does not decompose into factors. The reduced state of a part is then mixed~\cite{nielsenchuang2010}: the part has no branches of its own, its alternatives enter the branches of the whole, and a term exists only as a whole. The distinction goes back to Schr\"odinger: maximal knowledge of a whole need not include maximal knowledge of its parts~\cite{schrodinger1935}. In the information-theoretic formulation, the finite information of a composite system resides either in the properties of the parts or in their correlations~\cite{brukner2001}.

We call the weight of an interfering alternative a coherent probability. The product of such weights for separate systems or separate completed events we call a combinatorial probability: such is the probability of a joint string of a product, of a series of coin tosses, or of a chain of successive measurements. The quantum~$\varepsilon$ applies to coherent probabilities. A combinatorial probability can be arbitrarily small, because the threshold applies to each of its factors separately.

In the density matrix a coherent probability stands on the diagonal, while the coherence between alternatives is described by the off-diagonal elements. But not every diagonal element is a coherent probability. A density matrix can also be written for independent systems; for such a product the diagonal elements of the full matrix equal the products of the diagonal elements of the factors, that is, they are combinatorial probabilities: what interferes is the alternatives of each system separately. Coherent probabilities stand on the diagonals of the matrices of the individual factors; which elements are counted is set by the counting rule below.

Decomposability is not meant arithmetically. Any probability can be written as a product, for example through the conditional probabilities of the bits taken in turn. A factor counts only if it corresponds to the state of a separate system or to a separate completed event.

The counting rule is as follows. The state is written as a sum of products with the smallest number of terms. A factor that is not itself a product is written in the same way, and its terms are counted separately from the terms of the other factors. The weight of each term of this expansion is a coherent probability, and each is compared with~$\varepsilon$; the weight of a nested term is taken together with the weight of the term that contains it. The vanishing of a nested term reduces the weight of the term that contains it. If nothing is left of a term, it vanishes as a whole when the sum has other terms; if it is the only term, the degenerate case described below applies. The product $|+\rangle^{\otimes N}$ is one term, and each qubit has two branches of its own of weight~$1/2$; the probability~$2^{-N}$ of a string is combinatorial. A GHZ state has two terms of weight~$1/2$. The state in Grover's algorithm~\cite{grover1996} also has two: the sought string and the uniform superposition. A Haar state~\cite{page1993} has of the order of~$2^N$ terms of weight of the order of~$2^{-N}$; the calculations for it below use the strings of the pointer basis. If all coherent probabilities are not below~$\varepsilon$, the model predicts nothing new. The three cases are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:systems}.

It follows that entanglement has a smallest portion. In standard quantum mechanics any two systems that have ever interacted, even through intermediaries, remain slightly entangled, and there are no exact products. Here a term with weight below~$\varepsilon$ vanishes. Two systems are therefore either entangled by a term with weight not below~$\varepsilon$ or not entangled at all, and then their state is an exact product. The smallest possible entanglement of two systems is about $\varepsilon\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\approx10^{-118}$~bit. The entanglement of distant systems that has passed through many intermediaries falls below this portion and vanishes.

\paragraph{Vanishing of branches.}
A branch is a component in the einselection basis with diagonal weight~$p_i=\rho_{ii}$; the off-diagonal elements describe interference between branches. Physical interaction with the environment defines orthogonal sectors~$P_i$, not an arbitrary matrix representation. The number of sectors depends on how the environment is coupled to the system. If it acts on each qubit separately, the sectors are strings, and there are~$2^N$ of them. If it distinguishes only a total quantity, for example the total number of atoms in the upper level, there are $N+1$ sectors, and a branch is a value of this quantity, not a list of atoms. For a system weakly coupled to its environment the pointer states are the energy eigenstates~\cite{pazzurek1999}; the computational strings of an idle register are exactly such states. The \CollapseRule{} applies to the entire entangled system within which these records are linked.

The \CollapseRule{} tests one einselection object: one branch or the coherence of that branch with its complement. Let $P_i$ be the sector projector and $p_i=\Tr(P_i\rho)$ the Born weight of the branch. The elementary vanishing of a branch and elementary L\"uders dephasing~\cite{luders1950} take the form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{van}}_i(\rho)
=\frac{(I-P_i)\rho(I-P_i)}{\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((I-P_i)\rho\bigr)},
\qquad
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{ph}}_i(\rho)
=P_i\rho P_i+(I-P_i)\rho(I-P_i).
\end{equation}
Without the corresponding rows and columns, what remains is a principal submatrix of the positive semidefinite matrix~$\rho$, and L\"uders dephasing is completely positive. Therefore Hermiticity, positivity, and normalization are preserved~\cite{hornjohnson2013,luders1950}.

An object is absent from the description if its weight is smaller than the quantum.

\emph{Branch recorded by the environment.} A record in the environment does not make a branch an outcome: the system is entangled with the environment, and the branch belongs to the larger system that includes the environment. Over the whole budget of~$\Ost$ answers, branch~$i$ occurs on average $\Ost p_i$ times. If this is less than once, such a branch never occurs:
\begin{equation}
p_i<\varepsilon=\frac1{\Ost}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Coherent branch.} The same branch may enter a superposition with its complement, and then the probability of an outcome in another basis depends on the amplitude~$\sqrt{p_i}$ to the first power. With plain repetition this does not change the count: statistical noise falls as $1/\sqrt{\nu}$, and over~$\nu$ repetitions the amplitude is visible at $\nu p_i\gtrsim1$. But operations can also be spent coherently. Amplitude amplification~\cite{grover1996,brassard2002} raises the probability to order~$\nu^2p_i$ in~$\nu$ steps, so in standard quantum mechanics the budget~$\Ost$ would allow weights down to~$\varepsilon^2$ to be distinguished. The quantum of weight therefore does not follow from counting operations. It refers to the Born weight in the pointer basis by postulate: outcomes are what is registered, and the amplitude is a way to compute their probabilities. In the model, amplification does not rescue a small branch: the branch vanishes before it can be amplified (\hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}). A grid on amplitudes would give a threshold of~$\varepsilon^2$ and a wall around $2N_*\approx800$ qubits; experiment distinguishes these two variants.

The physically distinguishable objects are measurement outcomes: branch probabilities and the interference between them, recoverable from experimental statistics, including quantum tomography. The density matrix is only a description of this operationally accessible information, not an independent physical object; the observed state is a branch. The quantum of weight refers to each finest pointer sector of the entangled cluster, not to a sum of sectors: a sum of weights, each smaller than the quantum, is not recorded anywhere in the description. Therefore the vanishing of many such branches changes outcome probabilities noticeably: the total weight of the vanished branches can be of order unity. Experiment detects this change, that is, the very fact that the branches are absent; the weights of the vanished branches remain unmeasurable. There is no contradiction with the initial principle here: the principle is about the unmeasurability of the weight of an individual branch, not about its absence going unnoticed.

After the invisible branches vanish, several existing sectors with $p_i\ge\varepsilon$ generally remain. That is not a single outcome. The residual superposition of the full matrix in the einselection basis is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_P(\rho)=\sum_i P_i\rho P_i,
\qquad
C(\rho)=\frac12\bigl\|\rho-\Delta_P(\rho)\bigr\|_1.
\end{equation}
For two orthogonal branches with weights $p$ and $1-p$ one has $C=\sqrt{p(1-p)}$; for a Bell pair, $C=1/2$.

\paragraph{Irreversibility of the vanishing of branches.}
Since $\Ost(t)$ decreases monotonically, $\dot\varepsilon>0$: the threshold only rises with time. A branch that vanished at an earlier value of the threshold remains below it and, by \CollapseRuleD{}, is not restored. Unitary evolution may subsequently create a branch with the same quantum numbers, but it will be a new branch, not a restoration of the vanished one.

\paragraph{Renormalization.}
Let
\begin{equation}
\Sinv=\bigl\{i:\ p_i<\varepsilon\bigr\}
\end{equation}
be the set of invisible sectors. Only an existing branch can occur in a measurement. Therefore the probability of an outcome equals the share of its weight among the existing branches, provided their total weight is positive:
\begin{equation}
p'_i=\frac{p_i}{\sum_{j\notin \Sinv}p_j},\qquad i\notin \Sinv.
\end{equation}
The existing branches themselves do not change: their relative amplitudes and phases stay the same. Renormalization covers all existing branches of the entire entangled system. An independent cutoff of matrix cells by~$|\rho_{ij}|<\varepsilon$ is not applied: it may violate positivity~\cite{hornjohnson2013}, and the visible interference of one pair of sectors may add up from many cells within the blocks.

Let $\Pi=I-\sum_{i\in \Sinv}P_i$ be the projector onto the existing part of the state. For the full density matrix of the entangled system, the same statement is written in one step, as the truncation~$\mathcal N$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}(\rho)
=\frac{\Pi\rho \Pi}{\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi\rho \Pi)}.
\end{equation}

For $K$ equiprobable alternatives, each weight equals $1/K$. The weight of each existing branch is no smaller than the quantum, so the number of equiprobable alternatives is bounded:
\begin{equation}
K\lesssim\Ost,
\qquad
N_*\approx\log_2\Ost\approx400.4.
\end{equation}
In the estimates below it is convenient to use the round reference value~$400$.
This estimate refers to one equiprobable alternative within one connected system, not to a factorized product or to sequential classical recording. How to count branches for a product, GHZ, a Haar state, and RCS is described in \hyperref[sec:factorized]{\S~\ref*{sec:factorized}}.

\paragraph{Dephasing.}
Unitary entanglement with the environment moves inter-branch blocks into correlations and does not by itself reduce $C$ of the full~$\rho$ or raise its von Neumann entropy. For the coherence of the existing sectors to fall to~$\varepsilon$, dephasing of the full matrix is required; the mechanism may vary. As an example we take the hypothesis of independent proper-time clocks (BDPT)~\cite{ponfilenok2026bdpt}: the blocks $P_a\rho P_b$ decay as $e^{-\chi}$, $\chi=(E_a^2+E_b^2)\,\delta t^2/2\hbar^2$, where $E_a$ and~$E_b$ are the energies of those degrees of freedom in which the branches differ, and $\delta t$ is the uncertainty of proper time (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}). For masses separated in space, this is the rest energy~$mc^2$, and $\chi\propto(mc^2)^2t^{2/3}$; for the internal levels of a qubit, it is the transition energy. Radiation and $\Lambda$ energy do not enter the phase. As the blocks decay, the entropy of the full~$\rho$ grows toward~$S\bigl(\Delta_P(\rho)\bigr)$. The scales are as follows: for~$400$ qubits with a transition frequency of~$5$~GHz over~$1$~$\mu$s, $\chi\sim10^{-36}$, and coherence is unaffected; for a record of mass~$1$~pg separated over two branches, $C$ falls below~$\varepsilon$ in a time of order~$3\times10^{-18}$~s. This is a separate hypothesis, not a consequence of~$\Ost$. No numerical result of this work depends on it: the threshold, the wall, the limit on Shor's algorithm, and the local marginal test follow from truncation; the dephasing mechanism affects only the moment at which a single outcome appears.

\paragraph{Objectivation.}
\label{sec:objectivation}
Objectivation is a proper mixture of the full, unreduced~$\rho$: when $C(\rho)\le\varepsilon$, all off-diagonal blocks are zeroed, and the description is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_P(\rho)=\sum_i P_i\rho P_i.
\end{equation}
It is not an improper mixture obtained by tracing out the environment, in which a subsystem looks mixed while the global superposition still exists. It is a proper mixture of the full density matrix as a whole. A single outcome already exists but is unknown; this ignorance is the von Neumann entropy of the mixture, $S=-\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Delta_P(\rho)\log\Delta_P(\rho)\bigr)$. Thus objectivation is the growth of this physical entropy: $S\bigl(\Delta_P(\rho)\bigr)\ge S(\rho)$. Almost all of~$S\bigl(\Delta_P(\rho)\bigr)$ is accumulated by dephasing. At $C\le\varepsilon$ the act of zeroing itself adds at most of order~$\varepsilon\log K$, by the continuity of entropy~\cite{fannes1973,audenaert2007}, where $K\lesssim\Ost$ is the number of existing sectors. The mapping~$\Delta_P$ does not shift the weights~$\{p_i\}$: it removes from~$\rho$ the off-diagonal terms that are already invisible within the~$\Ost$ budget, and a Loschmidt echo~\cite{swingle2018} cannot restore them afterward. This is equivalent to coarse-graining by the distinguishability threshold: the cells are the existing einselection sectors.

The quantity~$C(\rho)$ is the residual coherence of the full matrix: a single trace distance to~$\Delta_P(\rho)$, including all blocks $P_i\rho P_j$ with $i\neq j$. Truncation is not required for this: when all branches are visible, $\mathcal N$ is the identity. If truncation has already left only one branch, the mixture is trivial and $S=0$. While $C>\varepsilon$, the difference between~$\rho$ and the mixture~$\Delta_P(\rho)$ is itself still visible within the~$\Ost$ budget, so interference is retained in the description and zeroing is not applied. For a branch it is its weight that is compared with the quantum, and for coherence it is the trace distance to the mixture; the choice of these two measures is part of the postulate.

\paragraph{Degenerate case.}
If \emph{all} branches are invisible, their joint vanishing would zero~$\rho$, and a proper mixture of invisible branches is inadmissible. Then selection acts,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sel}}(\rho)
=\frac{P_k\rho P_k}{p_k},
\qquad
\Pr(k\mid \Sinv)=\frac{p_k}{\sum_{i\in \Sinv}p_i}.
\end{equation}
This is not objectivation: the trigger is the absence of a visible remainder, not~$C\le\varepsilon$. This case arises for a flat distribution and determines the fate of Shor's algorithm (\hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}). If a system has at least one branch with a weight not below~$\varepsilon$, the case is not degenerate: the invisible branches vanish and that branch remains, however small its weight.

\paragraph{When the weight of a branch is defined.}
\label{sec:when}
The Born weight of a branch is the frequency of an outcome in an experiment that can detect this branch. An experiment is an operation with a completed result. Until the operation that creates a branch is completed, there is no such experiment: a measurement made in the middle of the operation is a different, shorter operation with its own branches and its own weights. The weight of a branch is therefore defined once the operation that created it is completed, and the condition $p_i\ge\varepsilon$ refers to these weights. The rule requires no separate act of comparison.

This removes the Zeno paradox~\cite{misra1977}. During an operation the weight of a new branch grows from zero, but until the operation is completed it is not a weight in the sense of the rule, and the branch does not vanish at birth. An operation cannot be shorter than the uncertainty of proper time, $\delta t\approx(t_P^2\,t)^{1/3}$, where $t$ is the superposition interval and $t_P=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^5}\approx5.4\times10^{-44}$~s is the Planck time~\cite{karolyhazy1966,ponfilenok2026bdpt}. An operation is completed when the interaction has carried the state into an orthogonal one. An ordinary entangling gate is one such operation; a part of a gate that does not bring the state to an orthogonal one is not an operation, so the count does not depend on how many pulses the gate is divided into. In one operation a new branch receives a fraction of the parent's weight of order unity. The exact formulation for arbitrary continuous evolution remains open (\hyperref[sec:discussion]{\S~\ref*{sec:discussion}}).

\section{Examples of the Rule}
\label{sec:scales}

\paragraph{Spatial tail of the $1s$ state.}
The probability density of the $1s$ state is $e^{-2r/a_0}/(\pi a_0^3)$, where $a_0$ is the Bohr radius. The characteristic momentum of the electron is~$\hbar/a_0$, the de~Broglie wavelength is $\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}=2\pi a_0\approx0.33$~nm, and the weight of a cell of this size at distance~$r$ from the nucleus is
\begin{equation}
w(r)\approx\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}^3}{\pi a_0^3}\,e^{-2r/a_0}=8\pi^2e^{-2r/a_0}.
\end{equation}
The condition $w<\varepsilon$ holds at $r\gtrsim7.5$~nm. Beyond that there are no branches: the electron will never be found there, whereas in standard quantum mechanics the probability is small but not zero. The result depends only weakly on the cell size: even for the finest possible cell, the Compton wavelength $h/m_ec\approx2.4$~pm, the boundary moves only to~$7.1$~nm. The total weight of the vanished cells is of the order of~$10^{-118}$, and the truncation does not affect the rest of the state. The tail vanishes both before the position is recorded by the environment and after it, as long as objectivation has not occurred (\hyperref[sec:objectivation]{\S~\ref*{sec:objectivation}}). If the interaction distinguishes not the position but the energy level, the ground state is one branch; for an isolated atom the two descriptions give the same observables to within this weight.

\paragraph{Factorized product.}
\label{sec:factorized}
For the factorized state $|+\rangle^{\otimes400}$, each qubit gives two outcomes of weight~$1/2$. Branches do not vanish, because each factor carries weight $1/2\gg\varepsilon$. The weight of a particular computational string of all~$400$ qubits, e.g.\ $|0\ldots0\rangle$, equals $2^{-400}$, but that is a combinatorial probability, a product of independent weights~$1/2$, not the weight of one branch.

\paragraph{Weakly entangled ensemble.}
In a cesium fountain, a cloud of millions of atoms in a superposition of hyperfine levels flies between the two Ramsey pulses for about half a second~\cite{wynands2005}. Collisions shift the phase of an atom depending on the states of its neighbors~\cite{gibble1993}, so the state of the cloud is not an exact product. The shift is small compared with the width of a Ramsey fringe, about~$1$~Hz: the phase~$\phi_{\mathrm{col}}$ accumulated per atom during the flight is much less than a radian, and the interference contrast of a single atom differs from unity by an amount of the order of $\phi_{\mathrm{col}}^2\ll1$. What interferes is the two levels of each atom, each of weight about~$1/2$ (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}); this is what is observed as the Ramsey fringes on which the clock operates. The probability~$2^{-N}$ of a joint string of the whole cloud, as in a product, is combinatorial.

\paragraph{Chain with short-range correlations.}
In the experiment of King et al., a chain of~$2000$ superconducting qubits is driven through a quantum phase transition in $5$--$50$~ns~\cite{king2022}. In the final state, neighboring qubits point the same way except at rare kinks, pairs of neighbors that point in opposite directions. At the fastest anneal the correlation length is $\xi\approx15$ qubits, and there is on average one kink per~$\xi$ pairs of neighbors: a probability of about~$0.066$. A typical complete result contains about~$130$ kinks, and its probability in the approximation of independent kinks is of order~$2^{-700}$, that is, below~$\varepsilon$. But stretches farther apart than a few~$\xi$ are not linked to each other, and neighboring ones are linked weakly: the kink correlations are reproduced by a calculation for a chain of~$256$ qubits in which no more than~$5$ bits of entanglement pass through a cut. What interferes is the alternatives of a stretch of length of the order of~$\xi$, with weights of order~$2^{-5}$, while the probability of a result of the whole chain is combinatorial (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}); nothing vanishes. Experiment agrees: the kink density at~$2000$ qubits is the same as at~$512$ and matches the calculation for a closed system. If the strings of the whole chain were interfering alternatives of one state, only results with at most~$53$ kinks would remain, in the same approximation.

\paragraph{Collectively coupled ensemble.}
In the experiment of Hosten et al., half a million rubidium atoms are coupled equally to the mode of a common cavity, and the spread in the number of atoms in the upper level is squeezed by~$20$~dB, that is, its variance is~$100$ times smaller than for independent atoms~\cite{hosten2016,hosten2016a}. The cavity distinguishes only this number, so the sectors are its values (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}), and the weight of each is of order~$10^{-2}$. Counting by lists of atoms would give weights far below~$\varepsilon$, and the squeezed state would have to vanish; in the experiment it is observed.

\paragraph{GHZ.}
For GHZ, all~$400$ qubits are entangled, but the global state is a superposition of only two strings, $|0\ldots0\rangle$ and $|1\ldots1\rangle$, each with weight $1/2$; one cannot sum over qubits. Branches do not vanish, because both existing branches carry weight $1/2\gg\varepsilon$.

\paragraph{Haar state.}
In a Haar state in dimension~$D=2^N$ there is no factorization: a particular computational string, e.g.\ $|0\ldots0\rangle$, is already a branch of typical weight $p_i\sim2^{-N}$, and of order $2^{N-1}$ such branches are distinguishable simultaneously. At $N\gtrsim400$ this exceeds~$\Ost$, and almost all strings have $p_i<\varepsilon$ and vanish; it is here that the ceiling $N_*\approx400$ applies, not in the factorized $|+\rangle^{\otimes N}$ or in GHZ.

\paragraph{RCS.}
Random circuit sampling (RCS)~\cite{boixo2018,arute2019} prepares a state close to Haar, so the same ceiling applies; the truncation front is given in \hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}.

\paragraph{Effective number of branches.}
We define the effective number of branches of an entangled system via the inverse participation ratio (IPR)~\cite{thouless1974,grobe1994}:
\begin{equation}
\Keff \;=\; \frac{1}{\sum_i p_i^2}.
\end{equation}
The normalization is $\sum_i p_i=1$; the sum of squares equals unity only if a single branch exists. For~$K$ equal weights $p_i=1/K$ one has $\sum_i p_i^2=1/K$ and $\Keff=K$, so the ceiling $K\lesssim\Ost$ may be saturated. For a Haar state in dimension~$D=2^N$ the situation is different:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i p_i^2\right]=\frac{2}{D+1},
\qquad
\Keff\approx\frac{D+1}{2}\approx2^{N-1}.
\end{equation}
The last equality uses the concentration of the IPR at large~$D$; in general the mean of the inverse is not the inverse of the mean.
After the branches with~$p_i<\varepsilon$ vanish, the surviving weights satisfy $p_i\ge\varepsilon$ and $\Keff\le\Ost$. If $\rho=\bigotimes_a\rho_a$, the condition applies to each factor separately: $\Keff(\rho_a)\le\Ost$; for a state that does not decompose into factors---$\Keff(\rho)\le\Ost$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\log_2\Keff\le N_*\approx400.4.
\end{equation}
In the product above each factor has $\Keff=2$, for GHZ~$\Keff=2$, and for a Haar state and RCS~$\Keff\approx2^{N-1}$. For a product the \CollapseRule{} applies to each factor separately (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}).

\section{Non-conservation of Energy at Collapse}
\label{sec:collapse-duration}

Truncation and selection are not unitary, so they do not guarantee conservation of the mean energy. The moment of the jump is defined no more sharply than the uncertainty of proper time (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}); on laboratory scales it is instantaneous.

\paragraph{Truncation energy.}
The mean energy of the state is $\langle H\rangle=\Tr(H\rho)$. After the invisible sectors $\Sinv=\{i:p_i<\varepsilon\}$ vanish, we have $\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\Pi\rho \Pi/\Tr(\Pi\rho \Pi)$, and the energy shift is defined as
\begin{equation}
\Delta E=\Tr(H\mathcal{N}(\rho))-\Tr(H\rho).
\end{equation}
This is not a new assumption but a consequence of the renormalization rule (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}). Let the sectors be approximately energy eigensectors, $[H,P_i]\approx0$ (this holds for the strings of an idle register, \hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}), and let $E_i=\Tr(HP_i\rho P_i)/p_i$ be the mean energy of branch~$i$. Then $\Tr(H\rho)=\sum_i p_i E_i\equiv\langle E\rangle$, $\Tr(H\mathcal{N}(\rho))=\langle E\rangle_{\bar{\Sinv}}$ and $\Tr(H\Pi\rho\Pi)=\sum_{i\notin \Sinv}p_i E_i$, where $\bar{\Sinv}$ is the complement of~$\Sinv$. Denoting $W=\sum_{i\in \Sinv}p_i$, $\langle E\rangle_{\Sinv}=(\sum_{i\in \Sinv}p_i E_i)/W$ and $\langle E\rangle_{\bar{\Sinv}}=(\sum_{i\notin \Sinv}p_i E_i)/(1-W)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:branch-energy}
\Delta E=W\bigl(\langle E\rangle_{\bar{\Sinv}}-\langle E\rangle_{\Sinv}\bigr)=\langle E\rangle_{\bar{\Sinv}}-\langle E\rangle.
\end{equation}
The shift is finite for any $W<1$ and does not exceed the spread of energies over the branches: $|\Delta E|\le W|\langle E\rangle_{\bar{\Sinv}}-\langle E\rangle_{\Sinv}|$. Under partial truncation it is nonzero only if the vanished and the existing branches differ systematically in~$E_i$. Truncation only removes branches, so at $[H,P_i]\approx0$ no energy values appear that were absent from the state: only the mean shifts. $H$ includes everything that exchanges energy with the system. In the local marginal test (\hyperref[sec:experiment]{\S~\ref*{sec:experiment}}) the scrambled arm carries on average about two hundred ancilla excitations, but the drive has given up the same amount in that arm; the total energies of the arms are equal, and the vanishing of an arm does not change this. Selection~$\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sel}}$ gives $E_k-\langle E\rangle$ in a single shot and zero on average over the Born distribution. In the degenerate case the mean energy after the act equals $\sum_i p_i E_i=\langle E\rangle$, i.e.\ $\langle\Delta E\rangle=0$ identically---by construction there is no systematic sink or source; in a single act only fluctuations of the order usual for the system remain. Whether the selection of an outcome is accompanied by an exchange of energy with the environment beyond this is not considered here (\hyperref[sec:discussion]{\S~\ref*{sec:discussion}}).

\section{Threshold Collapse in Quantum Computing}
\label{sec:pruning}

For a nearly equiprobable distribution, substantial truncation is expected as $\Keff$ approaches the ceiling $\Ost$. For a Porter--Thomas distribution~\cite{porter1956,boixo2018}, the rare tails cross the threshold earlier: already at $N=N_*$ one has $\Keff\approx2^{N-1}=\Ost/2$, and the vanishing weight is substantial. Therefore the exact RCS front below is determined directly by the weight distribution, not by the equality $\Keff=\Ost$.

\paragraph{RCS limit.}
For random circuit sampling (RCS)~\cite{boixo2018,arute2019}, the number of qubits~$N$ and the distribution width~$\Keff$ are critical. Let~$N$ be the number of qubits in the circuit and~$W=\sum_{i:\,p_i<\varepsilon}p_i$ the total Born weight of the vanishing branches. The computational basis of the processor is the pointer-state basis set by the definition of the qubit in its interaction with the environment~\cite{zurek2003,zurek2009}; the final readout only strengthens and registers the outcome in the same basis and does not create it. Laboratory isolation suppresses decoherence \emph{between} pointer states and preserves high fidelity, but does not abolish the pointer-state basis. After the entangling gates, the joint strings~$|x\rangle$ are einselection branches of the entire register with weights~$p_x=|\langle x|\psi\rangle|^2$, no longer reducible to a product of independent factors (\hyperref[sec:factorized]{\S~\ref*{sec:factorized}}). Truncation acts after each layer of gates (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}), before readout. For a coherent superposition of $K$ strings, $C$ is of order unity, not~$\varepsilon$: BDPT dephasing for qubits over microseconds is negligible ($\chi\sim10^{-36}$, \hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}). Readout fixes the already altered distribution; if many strings still exist at readout, a single outcome comes from selection at $C\le\varepsilon$ or the degenerate edge.

The converse question is whether residual noise pushes some of the qubits into the mode of sequential classical recording. An error event records which-path information about roughly one qubit and may entangle it with matter in the environment, in which case the rest energy of that matter enters the dephasing of the branch; then $\langle k\rangle = N d e_{2q}/2$. At the working point $N\approx400$, $d=40$, $e_{2q}=10^{-4}$, this is $\langle k\rangle\approx0.8$---less than one qubit out of four hundred, with the shift of the wall within the window $N\approx400\pm3$. The window in which the test works in terms of fidelity and the window in which leakage is negligible coincide. (Upper estimate: the coherent component of which-path recording error does not create it.)

For circuits on~$N$ qubits whose output distribution approaches Haar, the weights obey the Porter--Thomas distribution: $P(p)=2^N e^{-2^N p}$. The typical minimum weight is of order $p_{\min}\sim 2^{-2N}$. The condition $p<\varepsilon$ touches the first rare tails around $N\approx200$, but their total weight is negligible; a typical branch becomes invisible no later than $N\approx400$. The result also depends on the circuit depth~$d$---the number of sequential layers of two-qubit gates: for two-dimensional connectivity the Porter--Thomas distribution is established at the saturation depth $d_{\mathrm{sat}}\sim\sqrt{N}$, at which the light cone covers the entire chip; at $d\gtrsim d_{\mathrm{sat}}$ typical weights are already Haar, while in smaller circuits the vanishing of branches starts later.

Integrating the Porter--Thomas distribution up to the threshold~$p_{\mathrm{thr}}$ gives
\begin{equation}
W(u)
\;=\;
1-(1+u)e^{-u}
\;\simeq\;
\frac{u^2}{2},
\qquad
u=2^N p_{\mathrm{thr}}.
\end{equation}
Here the threshold is $p_{\mathrm{thr}}=\varepsilon$ and $u=2^{N-N_*}$. For a Haar state $u\approx2\Keff/\Ost$, so the condition $u=1$ corresponds to $\Keff\approx\Ost/2$, not to saturation of the formal ceiling. At a sensitivity $\delta=10^{-3}$ the effect becomes visible around $N=396$ qubits. At $N\approx400.4$ a weight $W=1-2/e\approx26.4\%$ vanishes; at $N=403$ it is already $W\approx98\%$.

It is important to distinguish the vanishing \emph{mass}~$W$ from the number of surviving \emph{strings}. The fraction of branches with $p_i\ge\varepsilon$ equals $e^{-u}$, whence
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{vis}} = D e^{-u} = 2^N \exp\bigl(-2^{N-N_*}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The limit $W\to1$ means only that almost all of the Born mass has gone into the tails below~$\varepsilon$, not that one string remains: at $N=405$ one still has $n_{\mathrm{vis}}\sim10^{111}$. After renormalization
\begin{equation}
\Keff = \frac{D(1+u)^2 e^{-u}}{u^2+2u+2}.
\end{equation}
For the Porter--Thomas distribution the model gives two markers tied to the position of the wall (below, for the upper bound): a soft truncation front at $N=N_*\approx400.4$ ($u=1$) and \emph{classicality}~$\Ncl$ at $n_{\mathrm{vis}}=1$, i.e.\ at
\begin{equation}
\Ncl\ln2 = 2^{\Ncl-N_*},
\qquad
\Ncl\approx408.5
\end{equation}
($\Keff\to1$; at $N=401$, $403$, $405$, $408$ we have $\Keff\approx2^{398.6}$, $2^{394}$, $2^{370}$, $2^{128}$). $\Ncl$ is the classicality of the \emph{pre-measurement state}: one pointer string remains. An ordinary firing of the apparatus is the other path: a few branches of large weight and $C\le\varepsilon$ after dephasing on the rest energy of the record. By the \CollapseRule{} all invisible branches vanish, even if their total weight is of order unity.

\paragraph{Shor algorithm limit.}
The influence of the threshold is determined not by the number of qubits as such but by the branching width in the pointer-state basis. The partition~$\{P_i\}$ is set by the environment and is therefore the same for the entire register: it is the same computational $Z$-basis as in RCS above. A periodic comb is a coherent superposition of pointer strings, not a pointer state, so it cannot be taken as a separate branch: that would be a representation convenient for the algorithm, not a partition set by the interaction with the environment (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}). Coarse-graining to combs would require the environment to record the second register and not record the first; meanwhile, the orthogonality of the second register is not by itself an irreversible classical recording.

Consider the stages of the standard Shor algorithm~\cite{shor1997}. Let~$m$ be the width of the counting register, $Q=2^m$, and $n$ the modulus to be factored. After the Hadamard transforms the first register remains a product of single-qubit states (the ``coherent product'' mode, \hyperref[sec:factorized]{\S~\ref*{sec:factorized}}), so $2^{-m}$ is a product of weights~$1/2$, not the weight of one branch; branches do not vanish. Modular exponentiation entangles the registers:
\begin{equation}
|\psi\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt Q}\sum_{k=0}^{Q-1}|k\rangle\,|a^k\bmod n\rangle,
\end{equation}
All~$Q$ pointer-basis strings carry equal weight $p_i=1/Q$. The factorizability of this state in the~$Z$-basis is determined by the order~$r$: the quantity $a^k\bmod n$ depends only on $k\bmod r$, so an exact decomposition into independent factors is possible if and only if $r\mid 2^m$, i.e.\ when $r=2^j$. In this case, setting $k=k_{\mathrm{hi}}\cdot2^j+k_{\mathrm{lo}}$, we obtain $|\psi\rangle=|+\rangle^{\otimes(m-j)}\otimes\frac1{\sqrt r}\sum_{k_{\mathrm{lo}}}|k_{\mathrm{lo}}\rangle|a^{k_{\mathrm{lo}}}\rangle$, and the factors have $\Keff$ equal to~$2$ and~$r$. For~$j<m$ all weights equal $1/2$ or $1/r$ and remain above~$\varepsilon$ as long as $r\le\Ost$ (i.e.\ $j\lesssim N_*$); for~$j=m$ the only weight is $2^{-m}$, and the wall is reached at $m\gtrsim N_*$, as for a flat distribution. As long as all weights are $\ge\varepsilon$, branches do not vanish. For~$r\ne2^j$ the state is non-factorizable. This is not enough: what matters is the number of terms (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}). For small~$r$ the state is a sum of a few products: the indicator of the congruence $k\equiv j\pmod r$ is a sum of~$r$ phase factors, each of which factorizes over the bits of~$k$, so there are at most~$r^2$ terms, their weights are of the order of~$r^{-2}$, and the wall does not fire. The number of terms is not less than the Schmidt rank between the counting and work registers, $\min(Q,r)$, so a large order is also needed: $\log_2 r$ of the order of~$m$. For this case the weights are counted below by strings: $\Keff=Q=2^m$ (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}). Hereafter we assume $r\ne2^j$ and $\log_2 r$ of the order of~$m$. For an RSA modulus $n=pq$ the order of a typical base is comparable to~$n$, and the fraction of bases~$a$ whose order is a power of two is inverse to the odd part of the exponent of the group $\mathbb{Z}_n^*$ and is negligibly small; moreover, at $r=2^j$ factoring does not require full period finding. Therefore the caveats do not weaken the conclusion about cryptographic moduli but are necessary for the correctness of the criterion.

The \CollapseRule{} is imposed during the circuit, not only on this final state. Modular exponentiation is implemented by sequential controlled-$U$ operations on one control at a time; after~$t$ steps $\rho=\rho_{t+\mathrm{target}}\otimes|+\rangle^{\otimes(m-t)}$, and the factors have $\Keff$ equal to~$2$ and~$2^t$. For~$r\ne2^j$ the entangled block carries $2^t$ equal branches of weight~$2^{-t}$; the unprocessed controls in~$|+\rangle$ are not yet touched (for~$r=2^j$ the block itself splits into $|+\rangle^{\otimes(t-j)}$ and an $r$-string part after $t>j$ steps, and the estimate below does not apply)---the global weight~$2^{-m}$ is not used here, since the state is factorized. For~$t>N_*\approx400.4$ all branches of the block are below~$\varepsilon$, the degenerate edge fires (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}), and the block collapses to one string $|k_0\rangle|a^{k_0}\rangle$; the algorithm breaks down already in the middle of the exponentiation. With further entanglement~$\Keff$ grows again, but the final limit remains $m\lesssim N_*$. The order~$r$ enters the criterion only through two conditions, $r\ne2^j$ and $\log_2 r$ of the order of~$m$; given these, the value of~$r$ itself does not enter the limit.

At the final step $t=m$ the distribution is flat, not Porter--Thomas, so the front is sharp: at $m\le400$ one has $p_i=2^{-m}\ge\varepsilon$ and all strings are visible, while at $m\ge401$ the condition $p_i<\varepsilon$ holds immediately for all of them. The second case is exactly the degenerate edge of the \CollapseRuleG{} (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}): the joint vanishing of all strings would zero~$\rho$, so one string $|k\rangle|a^k\rangle$ is preserved with the conditional Born probability. A single string carries no periodic structure, and the subsequent QFT gives an almost uniform spectrum instead of peaks at multiples of~$Q/r$. The value of~$r$ does not enter this condition beyond the assumptions already made.

For Shor's algorithm two conditions matter: the state of the counting and work registers does not reduce to a few terms (for period finding---$r\ne2^j$ and $\log_2 r$ of the order of~$m$), and the width of the counting register exceeds the wall; given the first condition, neither the order~$r$ itself nor the circuit size enters the criterion:
\begin{equation}
\log_2\Keff=m\lesssim N_*\approx400.4.
\end{equation}
The standard Shor algorithm for an $L$-bit modulus uses $m=2L$; if the strings of both registers are counted, it fails from $L\approx201$ ($m=402$). The reliable bound is weaker: the number of terms is not less than the number of values in the work register, that is, of the order of~$r$, and for an RSA modulus $r$ is of the order of~$2^L$, so the algorithm certainly fails at $L\gtrsim400$. This bound does not depend on the layout of the counting register: in the variant with one recycled control qubit~\cite{beauregard2003} there is no counting register, but the work register holds the same superposition of~$r$ values with weights~$1/r$. For RSA-512 ($m=1024$) it \emph{collapses} in the model.

However,~$m$ need not equal~$2L$. The Ekerå--Håstad schemes~\cite{ekera2017} reduce RSA factoring to a short discrete logarithm and use a counting register of width $m\approx L/2+L/s$ with a tradeoff parameter~$s$. For RSA-512 at $s\ge4$ this gives $m\le384<N_*$: if the wall lies at the upper bound, the string weight remains above the threshold and the modulus is factored; if $N_w\le384$, this scheme fails as well. The fate of RSA-512 therefore depends on the position of the wall; only standard period finding with $m=2L$ is certainly forbidden. Since $m\ge L/2$ for any~$s$, all known period-finding schemes for RSA-1024 and RSA-2048 fall in the zone $m>N_*$; the period-finding boundary lies around $L\gtrsim800$.

This is a prohibition on an algorithm, not on the problem. The same rule that breaks Shor's algorithm opens another path: search with verification. Let a register of~$\ell$ qubits be in a superposition of all candidates, and let a reversible circuit set a flag on the correct one. The verification itself does not touch the wall: it involves $2^\ell$ strings of weight~$2^{-\ell}$. On the candidates without the flag a scrambler mixes~$N_a$ ancillas. The candidate register is entangled with the ancillas through the flag: the state is a sum of two terms, the correct candidate with the ancillas in zeros, whose weight for a single correct candidate is~$2^{-\ell}$, and the wrong candidates with scrambled ancillas. In the second term all ancilla strings are below~$\varepsilon$; nothing is left of it, and it vanishes as a whole, together with the wrong candidates (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}). After renormalization the correct candidate remains. This is the mechanism of the local marginal test (\hyperref[sec:experiment]{\S~\ref*{sec:experiment}}), except that the weights of the arms are not $1/2$ and~$1/2$ but $2^{-\ell}$ and $1-2^{-\ell}$. There are two conditions. The correct candidate must be visible: $2^{-\ell}\ge\varepsilon$, that is, $\ell\le N_w$. The scrambled arm must vanish completely: the fraction of its weight that stays above the threshold, $(1+u)e^{-u}$ with $u=2^{N_a-N_w}$, must be much smaller than~$2^{-\ell}$. This takes about~$9$ ancillas more than the width of the wall: $N_a\ge409$ for the upper bound, where this fraction is~$2^{-536}$. This estimate is for a single truncation; with repeated truncations (\hyperref[sec:experiment]{\S~\ref*{sec:experiment}}) fewer ancillas suffice. The estimate is for an ideal circuit. If with probability~$p_{\mathrm{fail}}$ the scrambler fails and the ancillas of a wrong candidate stay in zeros, the wrong candidates keep a weight of the order of~$p_{\mathrm{fail}}$, and the selection must be repeated on fresh ancillas: about~$13$ times for $p_{\mathrm{fail}}=10^{-6}$ and $\ell=256$, that is, about~$5000$ ancillas, if the failures in the repetitions are independent. Appending qubits in the state~$|+\rangle$ to the wrong candidates is not enough: they remain independent systems, and the weight of a joint string is a product of independent weights, not the weight of a branch (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}).

The consequences are as follows. A key of up to~$N_w$ bits for which a check is known is found in a single run; this applies, for example, to AES-256 given known pairs of plaintext and ciphertext. An RSA-1024 modulus is factored by a search over the $256$ high bits of a factor: by Coppersmith's theorem~\cite{coppersmith1997}, knowledge of the high $L/4$ bits of a factor allows the modulus to be factored classically in polynomial time. This path works for $L/4\le N_w$, that is, no further than $L\simeq1600$. For RSA-2048 it needs~$512$ bits and fails: the correct candidate is itself below the threshold. This does not make RSA-2048 secure: other reductions of factoring to search are not examined here. It is known that nonlinearity and postselection give computational power that the standard theory lacks~\cite{abramslloyd1998,aaronson2005}.

\paragraph{General branching wall.}
A general prohibition, not tied to period finding, follows from the above. Any state of~$2^m$ terms (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}) with equal weights $p_i=2^{-m}$ and~$m\ge401$ is invisible as a whole: the degenerate edge fires (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}), and the register collapses to one string. Phase estimation (QPE) with a coherent counting register wider than~$N_*$ falls under the same condition if the target state is spread over~$d$ eigenvectors with $\log_2 d$ of the order of~$m$; Shor's algorithm is the special case $d=r$. The prohibition does not cover independent systems (\hyperref[sec:factorized]{\S~\ref*{sec:factorized}})---$|+\rangle^{\otimes N}$, the intermediate stages of Shor's algorithm---and narrow Ekerå--Håstad registers. Nor does it cover states of a few terms (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}): QPE on an eigenvector, where the counting register remains a product; amplitude estimation~\cite{brassard2002}, where there are two eigenvectors and two terms; Grover search~\cite{grover1996}, whose state at every step is a sum of two products, $|+\rangle^{\otimes N}$ and the sought string. For a \emph{flat} distribution the degenerate edge sets in immediately at $m\ge401$. In RCS, with the Porter--Thomas distribution, the situation is different: at $N=403$ already $W\approx98\%$, and at $N=405$, $W\to1$, but $n_{\mathrm{vis}}\sim10^{111}$ strings still survive; one string remains only around $\Ncl\approx408.5$. The soft front of the vanishing mass is steep in the window $N\approx400$--$405$; classicality is reached at~$\Ncl\approx408.5$.

This is the main falsifiable prediction of the model---stronger and closer to experiment than the consequence for Shor's algorithm: the mirror circuit, the local marginal test, and the RCS scan on one processor of about~$410$ qubits (\hyperref[sec:experiment]{\S~\ref*{sec:experiment}}) directly test the geometry of this wall, whereas Shor's algorithm with a counting register wider than~$N_*$ and a large order requires a fault-tolerant processor.

Experiment does not contradict the model even where analog simulators involve hundreds and thousands of entangled particles: the number of particles is not the number of branches. For a GHZ state of~$1000$ spins $\Keff=2$, not~$2^{1000}$. For an ensemble of independent or weakly interacting atoms, each in a superposition, the probability of a joint string is combinatorial (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}, \hyperref[sec:factorized]{\S~\ref*{sec:factorized}}): the number of joint strings~$2^N$ is not the number of branches. The \CollapseRule{} applies to coherent probabilities in the pointer basis of the environment (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}), not to arbitrary many-body entanglement. Ground states of magnets and superconductors are energy eigenstates, that is, one branch (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}). Coherent annealing on thousands of qubits and squeezed ensembles of $10^5$--$10^6$ atoms are treated in~\hyperref[sec:scales]{\S~\ref*{sec:scales}}: in the first case what interferes is the alternatives of short stretches, in the second the environment distinguishes only a total quantity. A direct conflict would arise upon demonstration of a state close to a Haar state on more than~$N_w$ qubits; the experiments known to us contain no such state.

The multiple-quantum NMR experiments in solids belong to the same series~\cite{krojanski2004,krojanski2006,alvarez2015}. In adamantane the evolution of the spins is reversed by pulses, and the number of correlated spins is determined from the returning signal; $650$, about~$4900$ and about~$7000$ spins have been reported. This quantity shows how far the correlation has spread, and the authors call it effective. The distribution of the weights of the strings is not measured in such experiments, and it is to this distribution that the threshold applies: what matters is not the number of entangled particles but the coherent probabilities, which depend on how close the state is to a Haar state. A GHZ state and a Haar state of the same number of spins give coherences of the same highest order, although the first has two terms and the second of the order of~$2^N$. The fraction of the signal that returns at the largest sizes is not given in these works. The model predicts that it must fall sharply when the number of strings with appreciable weight exceeds~$2^{N_w}$; a measurement of this fraction would test the model on existing setups.

In a fault-tolerant scheme there may be far more physical qubits than~$N_*$, but the limit concerns not their number but the logical structure of the algorithm---the width~$m$ of the logical counting register. Syndrome measurements record only errors in the environment and do not reveal the logical state (stabilizers commute with logical operators), so the period-finding strings are not by themselves converted to the classical recorded mode. For the same reason the estimate $\langle k\rangle$ does not apply to the logical register: correction does not let the environment accumulate a which-path recording of the logical state specifically.

\section{Experimental Test}
\label{sec:experiment}

The absolute value $\varepsilon\sim 10^{-121}$ is not accessible to direct measurement in the laboratory. We therefore propose to capture the geometry of the boundary, the break between two chains, and the nonlinear signal of the local marginal test---a shift of one qubit's probabilities without measuring the entire register. The scan runs over the number of qubits~$N$, the circuit depth~$d$, and the chain structure. These are not three independent threshold parameters: the \CollapseRule{} compares branch weights with~$\varepsilon$; $N$, $d$, and the structure only determine which distribution~$\{p_i\}$ the circuit prepares. In RCS after saturation~$d\gtrsim d_{\mathrm{sat}}$ the wall lies no higher than~$N\approx400$; a smaller circuit shifts it in~$d$, and narrow branching at the same~$N$ and~$d$ gives no wall. The model is refuted if a wide entangled state in the pointer basis behaves according to the standard theory at~$N$ well above~$N_*$. As long as processors grow and no wall appears, the window for~$N_w$ narrows from below.

\paragraph{Boundary geometry.}
Control under independent errors: the hyperbola $N\cdot d\approx\mathrm{const}$ (the error budget) is an approximation of the accumulation model, neither a consequence of standard quantum mechanics nor the threshold geometry of the \CollapseRule{}; the working control curve is built from the measured noise channels. Threshold wall model (\hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}): at $d<d_{\mathrm{sat}}$ the boundary depends on the depth, and after the Porter--Thomas distribution is established at $d\gtrsim d_{\mathrm{sat}}$ it bends toward the vertical. The bend lies at the position of the wall, for the upper bound in the region $N\approx400\pm3$. The front is $W=1-(1+u)e^{-u}$ with $u=2^{N-N_*}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:front}); $u$ doubles with each qubit. At $u\ll1$ we have $W\simeq u^2/2$, so $W$ grows roughly fourfold per qubit; in the transition window ($N\sim400$) the growth of $W$ slows. The formula refers to a single truncation. After saturation each further layer mixes the remaining strings anew, and truncation is repeated (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}). The front therefore depends on depth, but weakly: with each doubling of the number of layers after saturation it shifts down by about half a qubit. The position of the wall~$N_w$ is determined by the scan; the dependence on depth follows from the same formula applied repeatedly and is not tuned. The position of the bend should not follow a reduction of the ordinary error rate.

\paragraph{Mirror circuit.}
The idea is a random-circuit echo: a register prepared in $|0\rangle^{\otimes N}$ is run through the scrambler~$U$ and the reverse mirror~$U^\dagger$; under standard unitary evolution the state returns to zero, while under the \CollapseRule{} branches vanish inside~$U$ and the compensation breaks down. The metric is the return probability $F=\bigl|\langle 0|U^\dagger U_{\mathrm{phys}}|0\rangle\bigr|^2$~\cite{swingle2018}. Predicting~$F$ classically is not required: it suffices to run $U U^\dagger$ repeatedly on the chip and measure the fraction of returns to $|0\rangle^{\otimes N}$. Poisson statistics require $M\sim 100/F$ copies. Under independent stochastic two-qubit gate noise a rough estimate is $F\approx e^{-N d e_{2q}}$ (of order $N d/2$ gates in each of the two passes). At $e_{2q}=3\times 10^{-3}$, $d=40$, $N\sim400$ this is $F\sim10^{-21}$, which practically closes the test; at $e_{2q}=10^{-4}$ it is of order $0.20$. The testability condition is $e_{2q}\lesssim 5/(N d)$ (so that $F\gtrsim10^{-2}$). Randomized compiling~\cite{wallman2016} separates stochastic noise from coherent compensation. The sensitivity~$\delta$ of the mirror circuit sets the lower edge of the window: at $\delta=10^{-3}$ the vanishing weight becomes visible around $N=396$ qubits.

\paragraph{Discrimination from hardware noise.}
At fixed $N$, $d$, and circuit structure, the ordinary noise boundary should shift when the measured~$e_{2q}$ changes, whereas the position of the threshold front in~$N$ is set by~$\varepsilon$ and does not shift; lowering~$e_{2q}$ only changes the ability to see this front. Therefore repeating the scan on devices or calibrations with different~$e_{2q}$ is the main distinguishing test. Unknown correlated noise that does not follow the measured~$e_{2q}$ remains a competing explanation~\cite{kalai2016,rinott2022}.

\paragraph{Paired circuits.}
The comparison is made on one chip at the same~$N$ and the same depth~$d$; the circuits differ in chain structure, not in depth. The branching width is set by the number of terms of the state (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}) \emph{at each layer}, not by the number of entangled qubits. The wide circuit consists of random single-qubit rotations and two-dimensional layers of two-qubit gates (RCS). The superposition fills almost all~$2^N$ strings, and for the Haar distribution $\Keff\approx2^{N-1}$ after~$d\gtrsim d_{\mathrm{sat}}$. In the narrow circuit $\Keff$ is small \emph{throughout} the circuit. A classical reversible network (CNOT, SWAP on computational states) holds one string, $\Keff=1$. A CNOT chain after one~$H$ gives GHZ: all qubits are entangled, but there are only two existing branches, $\Keff=2$. The echo~$U^\dagger U$ is \emph{not} a narrow path: in the middle of the first~$U$ the distribution is already Haar, and the threshold fires before the unwinding. ``Narrow path'' means a small branching width, not a smaller~$d$ and not switching off some of the qubits. In the corresponding transition region the narrow circuit works normally; the difference between the two circuits reveals the geometry of the wall. The signature is a residual deviation of the wide circuit that does not disappear when ordinary errors are reduced. Isolation does not close the gap: chain structure helps, not only an increase of the relaxation and dephasing times~$T_1/T_2$ (the ordinary coherence scales of hardware qubits). The scan along the hyperbola $N\cdot d\approx\mathrm{const}$ is a separate control of the ordinary error-accumulation model (the noise budget is almost the same at different~$N$), not a difference in the depths of the two circuits; at each such~$(N,d)$ the systematics of the circuits differ.

\paragraph{Local marginal test.}
The circuit has $404$ qubits on one chip---the control~$\qC$, $402$ ancillas, and a qubit~$\qA$ entangled with~$\qC$ in a Bell pair. Qubit~$\qC$ is not measured. The bit is encoded by the operation: $Z$-control is a controlled-$U$; $X$-control is $H$ on~$\qC$, a controlled-$U$, and $H$ back. The conditional circuit on the ancillas is the identity at~$\qC=0$ and the scrambler~$U$ at~$\qC=1$; the mirror~$U^\dagger$ is not needed---the marginal is fixed at the moment of truncation. The metric is the marginal of qubit~$\qA$: $\rho_{\qA}=\Tr_{\qC,\mathrm{anc}}(\rho)$, i.e.\ $P(\qA{=}0)$ and $P(\qA{=}1)$.

In standard quantum mechanics both operations are local with respect to~$\qA$, and its marginal is strictly~$1/2$. In the model, under $Z$-control a fraction $W(2u)$ of the scrambled arm vanishes, and after renormalization
\begin{equation}
P_Z(\qA{=}1)=\frac{1-W(2u)}{2-W(2u)},
\qquad
P_X(\qA{=}1)=\tfrac12,
\end{equation}
where $u=2^{N-N_*}$. Under $X$-control the scrambled branches are symmetrically linked to $\qA=0$ and~$\qA=1$; the vanishing of branches shifts both outcomes equally. \paragraph{Implementation without conditional gates.}
\label{sec:unconditional}
A conditional scrambler requires every gate to depend on~$\qC$; implemented directly, this means auxiliary qubits to fan out the control and three-qubit gates. It is simpler to make the initial state conditional. Let the scrambler~$U$ be built from gates that conserve the number of excitations: two-qubit gates of the iSWAP type and single-qubit phase rotations. Then $U|0\ldots0\rangle=|0\ldots0\rangle$. First, qubit~$\qC$ flips half of the ancillas by a chain of CNOTs, as in the preparation of a GHZ state; then the same~$U$ acts unconditionally in both arms (Fig.~\ref{fig:circuit}). In the arm $\qC=0$ the ancillas stay all zero; in the arm $\qC=1$ the state spreads over the sector with~$N/2$ excitations, whose dimension is $\binom{N}{N/2}\approx2^{N-4.7}$. At $N=408$ this is $2^{403}$ strings: the same width as $402$ ancillas in the original scheme, with $P_Z(\qA{=}1)\approx5\times10^{-6}$; $410$ qubits are needed in total. Such a scrambler mixes more slowly than a Haar one, because excitations spread diffusively~\cite{khemani2018,rakovszky2018}; the saturation depth may exceed~$\sqrt N$, and a margin of a few ancillas makes the scheme insensitive to deviations from the Porter--Thomas distribution.

\paragraph{Control: block scrambler.}
The same scheme provides a control that distinguishes a coherent probability from a combinatorial one. Divide the ancillas into blocks and remove the gates between blocks from~$U$. With~$440$ ancillas and ten blocks of~$44$, the number of joint strings in the arm $\qC=1$ is~$2^{409}$, and the weight of each is below~$\varepsilon$. But at~$\qC=1$ the state of the ancillas is a product of the states of the blocks, and the probability of a joint string is combinatorial (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}): for each block the all-zero string has weight~$1/2$, the others about~$2^{-42}$, and nothing vanishes. The model predicts $P_Z(\qA{=}1)=1/2$ for the block scrambler and $P_Z(\qA{=}1)\to0$ for the full one on the same qubits. The number of strings is above the wall in both cases; the only difference is whether the state of the arm decomposes into factors.

\paragraph{Failure of standard Shor.}
The signature is QPE/Shor on a counting register of growing width~$m$: collapse no later than $m\approx400$ for a large order~$r$ that is not a power of two (\hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}). A base~$a$ with $r=2^j$ gives an extra null control: the counting register factorizes, and at $j\lesssim N_*$ all weights remain above~$\varepsilon$---the wall should not fire until the same~$m$ as for the factorized $|+\rangle^{\otimes N}$. A second null control is a small order~$r$: the state is a sum of a few products (\hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}), and the wall should not fire either. A pair of runs at the same $m$ and~$d$ with $r=2^j$ and with a large $r\ne2^j$ is the same differential test as the paired circuits above, but inside one algorithm. In the model, standard Shor certainly does not factor moduli longer than about~$400$ bits, and from $L\approx201$ bits upward if the strings of both registers are counted, whereas RSA-512 remains vulnerable to Ekerå--Håstad schemes with $m\le384$. The RCS transition is not shifted by hardware improvement: the front stays in place, no higher than $400$--$404$ qubits. For the local marginal test and the superluminal channel a processor of~$404$ qubits suffices with a conditional scrambler, and of about~$410$ in the scheme without conditional gates (\hyperref[sec:unconditional]{\S~\ref*{sec:unconditional}}). The competing explanation is correlated noise~\cite{kalai2016}: its boundary follows the error rate, whereas the threshold boundary is set by~$\varepsilon$. The statistical test of RCS and its dependence on the noise model are discussed in~\cite{rinott2022}. Comparison with GRW/CSL~\cite{ghirardi1986,ghirardi1990,bassi2013}: they have a plane of free parameters $(\lambda,r_c)$, and mass enters the spontaneous collapse rate; here the lower bound of the weight quantum, $\varepsilon=1/\Ost$, is not a fitted parameter, and mass enters only through BDPT. The structural choices---the einselection basis, renormalization (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}), and the width measure---remain.

\section{Superluminal Telephone}
\label{sec:signaling}

Once the local marginal test (\hyperref[sec:experiment]{\S~\ref*{sec:experiment}}) is confirmed, the same circuit is moved to a separated baseline of length~$l$: qubit~$\qA$, entangled with~$\qC$ by a Bell pair, is delivered to Bob in advance over telecom fiber, and the effect becomes a communication channel. The working point for the upper bound of the wall is $404$ qubits ($402$ ancillas; $\qC$ and~$\qA$ are not included in~$N$). Alice does not measure~$\qC$; the bit is encoded by $Z$- or $X$-control over the entire ensemble of~$M$ acts. At~$\qC=0$ the act is the identity; at~$\qC=1$ it is the scrambler~$U$. Bob measures~$\qA$ in the~$Z$ basis.

In ordinary quantum mechanics, Alice's local unitary operations on~$\qC$ and the ancillas do not change $\rho_{\qA}$ at Bob: when measuring~$\qA$ in the~$Z$ basis he always sees $P(\qA{=}1)=50\%$, regardless of Alice's choice between $Z$- and $X$-control. In the model, some global branches of the system $\qC+\text{ancilla}+\qA$ vanish, and probabilities are counted among the existing ones (truncation~$\mathcal N$, \hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}). That is the superluminal shift of the marginal. $Z$- and $X$-control yield \emph{different} full matrices~$\rho$, not two ensembles of the same~$\rho$. On Alice's isolated chip, $C$ does not fall to~$\varepsilon$ within microseconds: only the transition energy of the qubits enters the BDPT dephasing, and there are no copy-records. Selection at Bob fixes the already shifted bit. At $N=402$:
\begin{equation}
P_Z(\qA{=}1)=\frac{1-W(2u)}{2-W(2u)}\approx1.62\%,
\qquad
P_X(\qA{=}1)=\tfrac12,
\end{equation}
a difference of $\approx48.4$ percentage points. In ordinary quantum mechanics both probabilities equal $50\%$.

The signal arises precisely from the \emph{absolute} threshold. If the threshold depended only on relative weights, renormalization would not change Bob's observable probabilities. In GRW-type collapse models the averaged evolution is linear, so different preparations of one~$\rho$ are indistinguishable and the superluminal channel is closed~\cite{ghirardi1986,ghirardi1990,bassi2013}. For Weinberg's nonlinear quantum mechanics, Gisin and Polchinski showed that local operations on one side of an EPR pair change the statistics on the other~\cite{weinberg1989,gisin1990,polchinski1991}. Here~$U_Z$ and~$U_X$ are both local relative to~$\qA$, so in a linear theory~$\rho_{\qA}$ does not change under either; the nonlinearity of~$\mathcal{N}(\rho)$ violates precisely this preservation of the marginal (\hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}).

Bob's frequency of ones is the mean of $M$ coin tosses with probability~$q$; its width equals $\sqrt{q(1-q)/M}$. For the $\pm 2\sigma$ intervals around~$P_Z(\qA{=}1)$ and~$1/2$ not to overlap,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|P_Z(\qA{=}1)-\tfrac12\bigr|
\ge
2\Bigl(\sqrt{P_Z(\qA{=}1)\bigl(1-P_Z(\qA{=}1)\bigr)/M}+\sqrt{1/(4M)}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Hence at $N=402$ we obtain $M\approx7$ acts per bit. Each act is a direct controlled-$U$ on the ancillas at~$\qC=1$ (at~$\qC=0$ it is the identity). On a two-dimensional lattice, saturation takes $d_{\mathrm{sat}}\sim\sqrt{N}\approx20$ layers (\hyperref[sec:pruning]{\S~\ref*{sec:pruning}}), which at a layer duration of tens of nanoseconds gives $t_{\mathrm{run}}\sim0.5$~$\mu$s per act; one bit then takes a few microseconds, and the transmission rate is of the order of hundreds of kbit/s. All numbers in this section are order-of-magnitude estimates. They refer to the scrambler itself and do not include the conditional preparation (of the order of~$\sqrt N$ layers) or a possible increase in depth (\hyperref[sec:unconditional]{\S~\ref*{sec:unconditional}}); this is an upper bound on the rate. Each act consumes one Bell pair~$\qC$--$\qA$ (after the readout of~$\qA$ new entanglement is needed), so a stable channel requires a pair distribution rate $R_{\mathrm{Bell}}=1/t_{\mathrm{run}}\sim10^6$ pairs/s; at a lower~$R_{\mathrm{Bell}}$ the real transmission rate is limited by~$R_{\mathrm{Bell}}/M$, not only by~$t_{\mathrm{run}}$ on the chip. The readout of qubit~$\qA$ at Bob takes nanoseconds and is not included in~$t_{\mathrm{run}}$.

The superluminal signal itself is a truncation jump (\hyperref[sec:collapse-duration]{\S~\ref*{sec:collapse-duration}}), simultaneous on the FRW layer (\hyperref[sec:comoving]{\S~\ref*{sec:comoving}}), not the continuous $\chi(t)$. The channel beats light if $M\cdot t_{\mathrm{run}}<l/c$; the minimum baseline is of the order of a kilometer. The local marginal test remains on one $404$-qubit chip (a baseline of centimeters); the race with light is set by separating~$\qA$ and Alice's station by at least that distance after the effect is confirmed. Synchronization of control and readout on a unified comoving time scale is described in~\hyperref[sec:comoving]{\S~\ref*{sec:comoving}}.

\paragraph{Two-way communication.}
Two-way communication is possible with symmetric $404$-qubit setups at each station: each has $402$ ancillas and its own half of a Bell pair~$\qC$--$\qA$. In an Alice$\to$Bob tick, $\qC$ and the ancillas are at Alice and $\qA$ is at Bob; in a Bob$\to$Alice tick the roles swap. $\qC$ and~$\qA$ are the control and the marginal readout, not ``Alice's and Bob's qubits.'' One act consumes a pair: after the readout of~$\qA$ new entanglement is needed, and the same pair is unavailable for the next bit. In the active tick the pair-source station must provide the same~$R_{\mathrm{Bell}}\sim10^6$ pairs/s. With one pair one cannot transmit and receive simultaneously---one either speaks or listens. Opposing ticks are separated on the comoving layer (\hyperref[sec:comoving]{\S~\ref*{sec:comoving}}), not launched simultaneously. Other engineering assumptions are given in \hyperref[sec:discussion-signaling]{\S~\ref*{sec:discussion-signaling}}.

\section{CMB Frame}
\label{sec:comoving}

The model \emph{assumes} that the quantity~$\Ost$ and the simultaneity of the truncation and selection jumps are set not by laboratory clocks but on a layer of constant cosmological time of the homogeneous isotropic Friedmann--Robertson--Walker (FRW) metric---the standard geometry of the expanding Universe. In this idealization the layer $t_{\mathrm{FRW}}=\mathrm{const}$ is one and the same for the entire metric: all comoving observers (zero peculiar velocity) agree on simultaneity. In practice, the same preferred frame is the rest frame of the cosmic microwave background (CMB); it is to this frame that the model attaches the jump, not to the local simultaneity of each laboratory and not to the continuous $\chi(t)$ of the BDPT hypothesis. A setup on Earth moves relative to the CMB at a speed of order $370$~km/s, so its Einstein or GPS synchronization is shifted from this layer by $vl/c^2$ (see below). Entangled pairs are separated subluminally and remain inside one causal patch, so the operation budget is defined over the entire experiment. The jump is simultaneous on this layer in the idealization of a homogeneous FRW metric; in inhomogeneous or rotating geometries a global comoving layer is not naturally defined. Rest relative to the CMB defines a preferred frame; compatibility with the constraints on Lorentz-invariance violation~\cite{mattingly2005,liberati2013} is not discussed here. The open questions of extending the CMB layer to local systems, of the preferred frame, and of causal consistency are discussed in \hyperref[sec:discussion-comoving]{\S~\ref*{sec:discussion-comoving}}.

\paragraph{Preferred-layer test.}
\label{sec:comoving-test}
The laboratory moves relative to the CMB at a speed $v\approx370$~km/s. The shift $vl/c^2$ exceeds one readout time~$\tau$ at $l\gtrsim(c^2/v)\tau$; for $\tau\sim100$~ns this is $l\gtrsim24$~km. The events are launched \emph{deliberately simultaneously} in the laboratory. Then the orientation of the baseline relative to the CMB dipole decides whose recording crosses the FRW layer first, and Bob's statistics should oscillate with the \emph{sidereal} period ($\approx23$~h~$56$~min), not the solar day. Solar motion (temperature, grid) is filtered out by this period.

The Alice$\to$Bob channel is not switched off: if Alice launches the measurement earlier than Bob in the laboratory by a margin greater than~$vl/c^2$ (at $24$~km this is~$\sim100$~ns), she is also earlier on the FRW layer at any time of day.

This is a separate protocol, not a race with light. The goal is to verify that the preferred layer coincides with rest relative to the CMB. Laboratory simultaneity is set by Einstein synchronization: a light pulse makes a round trip, and the midpoint of the flight is the common tick; in practice GPS/GNSS or two-way fiber transmission does the same. Such synchronization is tied to Earth time, not to the CMB: the difference between the layers is precisely the shift $vl/c^2$.

\paragraph{Synchronization by comoving clocks.}
The model postulates the simultaneity of collapse on the CMB layer. Since laboratory clocks do not coincide with CMB-layer simultaneity, to synchronize by the absolute collapse time, local clock readings must be converted to the cosmological time~$t_{\mathrm{FRW}}$. Alice and Bob first synchronize their atomic clocks in the usual way, then convert proper times using the known worldlines of the stations and a metric model. For nearby stations on Earth, the main simultaneity correction to first order equals $\Delta t_{\mathrm{CMB}}\simeq\mathbf{v}\!\cdot\!\Delta\mathbf{x}/c^2$, which accounts for the velocity and the orientation of the baseline relative to the CMB. If, for example, Alice is on Earth and Bob is in open space, this correction alone is insufficient: in a weak field the local clock rate is related to comoving time by
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\tau}{dt_{\mathrm{FRW}}}\simeq1+\frac{\Phi}{c^2}-\frac{v^2}{2c^2},
\end{equation}
so along each worldline one integrates the gravitational potential~$\Phi$ and the kinematic time dilation separately. In addition, one accounts for the Earth's rotation and orbital motion, the trajectory of the space station, the propagation delay of the calibration signals, and local perturbations of the metric.

On this common scale one can schedule the control and readout moments of the superluminal channel in advance. Such synchronization is especially convenient at large distances, including distances in space: both stations use one cosmological time coordinate rather than determining the order of remote events by exchanging light signals during the session. For a baseline of $\sim1$~km the maximum kinematic correction is about $4$~ns, small compared with an act of $\sim0.5$~$\mu$s but available for calibration; at a space baseline the combined kinematic and gravitational correction becomes substantial. This is not a separate physical signal of global clocks but a conversion of local clock readings to the assumed preferred frame; its accuracy is limited by the precision of the ephemerides, the model of the gravitational field, and the extension of the CMB layer to inhomogeneous geometries (\hyperref[sec:discussion-comoving]{\S~\ref*{sec:discussion-comoving}}).

\section{Discussion and Open Questions}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Integer micromodel.}
The postulate of limited description and a sketch of the integer grid $n_i/\Ost$ are given in \hyperref[sec:rules]{\S~\ref*{sec:rules}}. A full microtheory has not yet been derived from this sketch. The weights of a product of subsystems, of the form $n_i m_j/\Ost^2$, do not lie on the grid, but they are combinatorial probabilities and need not lie on it (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}). Open obstacles: as $\Ost(t)$ decreases (\hyperref[sec:cosmological-limit]{\S~\ref*{sec:cosmological-limit}}) the entire discrete scale of distinguishability is rebuilt; the moment at which the weight of a branch is defined is fixed for operations with a definite completion (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}) but is not defined for continuous evolution without distinguished operations. Until these are resolved, the \CollapseRule{} specifies an effective one-step rule on the full density matrix of an entangled system, implementing the postulate for einselection objects, not a closed discrete quantum mechanics with an unambiguous evolution~$\rho(t)$.

\paragraph{Dependence on depth and the jump at the threshold.}
After each completed operation the truncation is repeated (\hyperref[sec:when]{\S~\ref*{sec:when}}), so the result depends on the depth of the circuit; this is a prediction of the model, and the dependence on the number of layers can be measured (\hyperref[sec:experiment]{\S~\ref*{sec:experiment}}). The count does not depend on how many pulses a gate is divided into. For arbitrary continuous evolution the rule is not specified. The rule is discontinuous: a branch with weight just below~$\varepsilon$ vanishes, and one just above it remains. For a single branch the jump in probabilities is of the order of~$\varepsilon$ and unobservable; when many branches cross the threshold, their total weight changes smoothly, according to the front formula. A finite discontinuity remains at the edge of the degenerate case: if all branches are below the threshold, a random string is selected, and if one of them is just above it, that one remains. This discontinuity follows from the quantization of weight.

\paragraph{Local Bekenstein bound.}
In this work the threshold is determined by the global causal-patch budget and is the same for all entangled systems. An alternative microtheory is possible in which the available precision is additionally limited by the Bekenstein bound $S\le 2\pi ER/(\hbar c)$ for a region containing the physical carriers of the entanglement~\cite{bousso2002}. For a compact system this local memory limit may be stricter than the cosmological operation budget; then $\varepsilon$ and the critical qubit number $N_*$ will depend on the size and energy of the setup. Whether a quantum state should be treated as locally stored information or as information of the entire causal patch remains an open question.

\paragraph{Palmer invariant set.}
A related idea is Palmer's invariant set~\cite{palmer2009}: reality is limited to a fractal invariant set. Here the limitation is different: the operational distinguishability of states within a causal patch, not the geometry of an attractor.

\paragraph{Branches.}
An absolute threshold requires unambiguously defined branches. The difficulties of defining and counting them are discussed by Saunders~\cite{saunders2021}. There is as yet no generally accepted precise definition of a branch~\cite{riedel2025}; definitions through redundant records~\cite{riedel2017} and through complexity~\cite{weingarten2022,taylor2025} have been proposed. Here probabilities are set by the absolute Born weights $p_i=\Tr(P_i\rho)$ in the einselection basis, not by the number of branches; the rule is a function of a single~$\rho$. The contrast between $Z$- and $X$-control in the local marginal test and in its separated realization arises not from two decompositions of one~$\rho$ into ensembles but from different unitary operations that change~$\rho$ itself; the dependence on the coarse-graining level remains an open question. In practice, the weight threshold acts on the pointer strings of the full matrix, while $C$ and BDPT act on already truncated sectors; whether these partitions always coincide has not been shown. For many weakly coupled systems the expansion with the smallest number of terms is not written out here: the counting rule and both limits are given (\hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}). The geometric measure of entanglement~\cite{wei2003} can serve as a measure of the distance from a product. It is also undecided whether the terms are set by the environment or by the state itself: for Haar states the answers coincide, while in general they may differ. In annealing experiments on two- and three-dimensional lattices~\cite{king2023,king2025} the link between parts grows with the area of the cut. In the 2025 work the lattices contain up to~$567$ qubits, and the state is reproduced to the accuracy of the processor itself, by the authors' estimate, at a bond dimension of $10^{7}$--$10^{11}$, that is, at $23$--$37$ bits through a cut; the terms of the Schmidt decomposition at the cut carry a weight far above~$\varepsilon$. These data do not determine the number of terms of the whole state.

\paragraph{BDPT and the budget~$\Ost$.}
Proper-time dephasing and selection itself at $C\le\varepsilon$ are not derived from~$\Ost$: the threshold sets visibility only. Which matter enters the energy of a branch is a separate input. If all matter with which the system is even slightly entangled is counted, the system spreads to the horizon. In~\cite{ponfilenok2026bdpt} a narrower rule is adopted: only the energy of those degrees of freedom in which the branches differ enters the dephasing. For an apparatus that has recorded the path, both rules give the rest energy of the record. Two experiments linked by infinitesimal entanglement are formally described by one~$\rho$. Their state is a product plus small terms. The terms with weight below~$\varepsilon$ vanish by the same rule; the rest are themselves products, and within each the experiments are counted separately (the smallest portion of entanglement, \hyperref[sec:systems]{\S~\ref*{sec:systems}}).

\paragraph{CMB frame and causality.}
\label{sec:discussion-comoving}
In \hyperref[sec:comoving]{\S~\ref*{sec:comoving}} the simultaneity of the truncation and selection jumps is postulated on a layer of constant FRW time; on the cosmological background it coincides with the CMB rest frame. The continuous $\chi(t)$ is not tied to this layer. For an inhomogeneous gravitational field, no unambiguous extension of this layer from the cosmological background to local Earth and spacecraft systems is defined. The conversion of the stations' proper times to~$t_{\mathrm{FRW}}$ depends on the worldlines, the ephemerides, and the metric model; the required precision and an experimental way to test the preferred frame remain open. The preferred layer sets a single order of remote events and may thereby exclude causal loops arising under Lorentz-invariant superluminal transmission only if all nonlinear dynamics strictly obey this foliation; the causal consistency of the full microtheory has not yet been shown.

\paragraph{Non-conservation of energy.}
Formula~\eqref{eq:branch-energy} gives the exact shift of the mean energy under truncation; no new energy values appear in the process (\hyperref[sec:collapse-duration]{\S~\ref*{sec:collapse-duration}}). In the degenerate Born choice the mean shift vanishes, unlike in GRW/CSL models~\cite{ghirardi1986,ghirardi1990,bassi2013}, where the parameter~$\lambda$ is limited by spontaneous heating. The energy balance at the selection of an outcome is a topic for a separate article.

\paragraph{Objectivation only.}
The postulate also admits a narrower rule: invisible branches do not vanish and probabilities are not renormalized, and at $C\le\varepsilon$ only objectivation~$\Delta_P$ acts. The superluminal channel (\hyperref[sec:signaling]{\S~\ref*{sec:signaling}}) is then closed: the marginal shift is produced by the mapping~$\mathcal N$, not by the proper mixture. The working model takes truncation and objectivation; which of the mappings enters the dynamics remains open.

\paragraph{Superluminal telephone engineering.}
\label{sec:discussion-signaling}
The scheme in~\hyperref[sec:signaling]{\S~\ref*{sec:signaling}} gives a preliminary architecture and working estimates for a direct controlled-$U$, without the cost of conditional control (\hyperref[sec:unconditional]{\S~\ref*{sec:unconditional}}). The engineering bottleneck is not~$t_{\mathrm{run}}$ on the chip but the Bell-pair pipeline: at~$M\approx7$ and~$t_{\mathrm{run}}\sim0.5$~$\mu$s one needs a rate~$R_{\mathrm{Bell}}\sim10^6$ pairs/s per direction, orders of magnitude above that of typical quantum communication lines. A possible optimization is to prepare $U|0\rangle$ offline on a buffer register in parallel with the previous act and, in the tick, to substitute it at~$\qC=1$ by a controlled-SWAP (at~$\qC=0$ the ancillas remain in~$|0\rangle$). In standard quantum mechanics this is equivalent to controlled-$U$ at the level of the final global~$\rho$; for nonlinear \CollapseRuleG{} the equivalence requires a separate assumption that no threshold event occurs during the preparation and storage of the prefabricated state. If such an optimization is admissible, the act duration shrinks to $t_{\mathrm{run}}\sim50$~ns (loading the prefabricated state and $Z$/$X$-control on~$\qC$, without rerunning the $d_{\mathrm{sat}}$ layers), the transmission rate rises to a few Mbit/s, and the minimum baseline shrinks to the order of a hundred meters, but~$R_{\mathrm{Bell}}$ rises to~$\sim10^7$ pairs/s. In the nonlinear model the final state by itself may not determine the outcome: the threshold may fire already during the offline preparation, so the equivalence of the prefabricated state and controlled-$U$ must be specified by the microdynamics and tested experimentally. Also open are the resources and duration of the multi-qubit controlled-SWAP, buffer reset, pair generation and delivery at the rate~$R_{\mathrm{Bell}}$, Bell-pair preservation, and readout synchronization.
